## F. README claims checked

Section 2 and 3 literature statements. Every quote below is from a text I opened (PDF or XML text extracted locally), except where "abstract only" is stated.

1. **Storer 2023: COSEG numbers subfamilies arbitrarily, no regard to stepwise accumulation, each primate genome gets its own list.** CONFIRMED. Introduction, para 2 (PDF p. 2): "However, the COSEG output assigns subfamily names arbitrarily, starting with subfamily "0" and going through to subfamily x, in no particular order and with no regard for the step-wise accumulation of diagnostic mutations." Abstract: "this naming convention can become increasingly confusing as more genomes are independently analyzed." The words "lineage by lineage" are the README's paraphrase; the paper says COSEG "identified entire lineages of subfamilies simultaneously" (Abstract), per genome. The paper's journal data: Genes 14:249 (checked from the PDF title block and Europe PMC).
2. **"More than 10 % of replicate copies change RepeatMasker subfamily between runs (Carey 2020)".** WRONG as worded; the finding is real. Reference is Carey, Patterson, Wheeler, Mobile DNA 12:4 (2021); the 2020 item is the Research Square preprint. The 10 % is not "between runs": it is between two copies of the same insertion (segmental-duplication pairs in human, and human-chimp homologous pairs), each annotated once. Abstract: "We find that standard methods annotate over 10% of replicates as belonging to different subfamilies, despite the fact that they are expected to be annotated as belonging to the same subfamily." Results, segmental duplications: "The extent of discordant annotation varied among families (Table 1), but was high in both younger TE families (Alu, 12.4%) and older families (L1, 14.1%)." (16,962 pairs in all families.) Whether RepeatMasker gives different results when run twice on the same copy is not what is measured. I did not find the human-chimp Alu percentage in the extracted text (it is in the table).
3. **Wacholder 2014: many subfamilies contain several source elements; consensus-based ages need reassessment.** CONFIRMED. Abstract: "computational and experimental work indicates that many of the subfamilies generated by these methods contain multiple source elements." and "Our results strongly indicate that a reassessment of subfamily structures is necessary to obtain accurate estimates of mutation processes, phylogenetic relationships and historical times of activity." Discussion: "this prior widespread use of subfamily consensus sequences as the single ancestral subfamily source sequence to analyze TE mutation patterns [14] has led to over-estimation of substitution rates and TE divergence times". Note: the data are LAVA (gibbon) and human AluSc only; Results: "57 LAVA sequences (6.5%) and 19 Alu sequences (2%) are assigned to different subfamilies than their most probable ancestor."
4. **Konkel 2015: active human subfamilies keep being added, AluYb7a3, AluYb8b1, AluYa4a1.** CONFIRMED. Abstract: "Alu Ya5 is currently the most active Alu subfamily in the human lineage, followed by Alu Yb8, and many others including three newly identified subfamilies we have termed Alu Yb7a3, Alu Yb8b1, and Alu Ya4a1." Volume 7, first page 2608 confirmed in the XML.
5. **Alu is about 10.09 % of T2T-CHM13 (308 Mbp), table in PMC13604091.** CONFIRMED. Biomolecules 2026, Table 4 "TE content of the GRCh38 and T2T reference genomes. Data from [16] are rounded." row: "Alu 10.43 304 10.09 308". Reference [16] is Hoyt 2022. Primary value: Hoyt 2022 Table 2 (supplement table in the author manuscript): "Alu 308,309.7 10.0926 304,734.4 10.4283" for CHM13v1.1 RMv2 and GRCh38 RMv2. These are v1.1 annotations (the audit uses v2.0).
6. **Halabian 118,489.** FOUND (section B). Abstract: "We analyzed 118,489 full-length AluY subfamilies annotated within the first complete human reference genome, T2T-CHM13." Results (p. 7): "The initial count of AluY subfamily members from the RepeatMasker output (CHM13v2.0_RM-2022MAR23.out) provided by the T2T consortium was 166,483. However ... we extracted only 128,695 full-length AluYs ... we excluded the full-length AluYs that were located within segmental duplication regions ... Consequently, our analysis comprised 118,489 full-length AluYs". Table 1: human 166,483 / 118,489 / TSD 118,157. Thus 118,489 is full-length AluY outside segmental duplications, not all full-length AluY (128,695).
7. **Xing 2004: published Alu CpG : non-CpG ratio about 6.** CONFIRMED, abstract only (full text paywalled). "Our results indicate a relatively constant CpG versus non-CpG substitution ratio of approximately 6 for the young (AluY) and intermediate (AluS) Alu subfamilies." It is a ratio of substitution density from 5,296 elements in 20 subfamilies. The 5.8 measured value comes from the other repository and was not checked.
8. **Noyes: 73 children, 42 families, 6,030 validated de novo SNVs, T2T-CHM13v2.0.** CONFIRMED (published and preprint). Abstract: "73 children from 42 autism families (157 individuals)"; Results: "With T2T-CHM13v2.0 as a reference genome ... Ultimately, 6030 de novo SNVs passed our validation criteria". Methods: "a final callset of 6030 autosomal SNVs".
9. **Alu germline enrichment p = 9.93e-6, not in L1, in the preprint.** CONFIRMED in both.
